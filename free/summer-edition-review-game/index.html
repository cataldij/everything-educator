HOW IT PLAYS

Split the class into three teams. Each round runs in two parts.

Part one: questions. Six editable questions. Any team can buzz in. Each correct answer earns one guess for that team. After Q6, the leaderboard locks in.

Part two: the cake reveal. Six summer-themed numbered items appear on the stage. ONE is a real cake. FIVE are not. The leading team picks first and gets up to three guesses. Second place gets two. Third place gets one. Click a number. Wrong = NOT CAKE! Right = IT'S CAKE! and that team scores the round.

Three rounds. Highest total wins. Game time: 30 to 45 minutes.

PowerPoint AND Google Slides, Platform Notes

This game works in both PowerPoint AND Google Slides, with one important platform difference to know about:

PowerPoint version (.pptx file in your download):

  • All click-to-reveal animations work natively, auto-disappearing obstacles, letter-flip reveals, card-reveal effects, etc.
  • Press F5 to enter presentation mode and play. Everything triggers automatically on click.

Google Slides version (copy link included):

  • Google Slides does NOT preserve PowerPoint’s click-trigger animations when it converts a .pptx file. This is a Google Slides platform limitation, not specific to this game.
  • The good news: all the click-to-navigate gameplay works perfectly in Google Slides presentation mode, clicking obstacles/cards/letters takes you to their question, clicking through to answers works, hyperlinks all fire normally. Only the “auto-disappear after cleared” visual feedback is missing.
  • The Google Slides version includes a dedicated “How to Play in Google Slides” directions slide (slide 3) explaining how to track cleared items: a whiteboard checklist, the editable scoreboard slide, or just verbally (many teachers find this adds suspense).
  • All scoreboard, team rosters, and editable text fields work identically in both versions.

Our recommendation: If you have PowerPoint, use the .pptx version for the smoothest experience with automatic animations. If you primarily use Google Slides, the click-to-navigate game flow works smoothly, you’ll just track cleared items separately on a whiteboard, which many teachers prefer because it keeps students more engaged in the strategy.
