Why the files look like this

Before returning to full time teaching, Jon served as Director of AI and Technology for an international K to 12 school network. Part of that job was watching good material fail in practice, usually for boring reasons. The font was not installed. The name was wrong and could not be fixed. The color version ate a cartridge and there was no black and white version. The file assumed a device every kid had, right up until the wifi went down.

So the rules here are simple and they do not change. Everything is editable in PowerPoint and Google Slides. Everything prints in color and in black and white. Nothing needs a font you have to install. Nothing needs a device.

The Spanish side

A good part of the store is written in Spanish, for Spanish class, by the person teaching it. That matters more than it sounds. Most Spanish classroom decor is English decor run through a translator, and students notice the difference between language that was written and language that was converted.

How a file gets published

Four steps, in this order, every time. The order is the point.

  1. Built for one classA specific group, a specific week, a specific thing that was not working.
  2. Used with studentsIn the room, on the day, with whatever the room does to a plan.
  3. Fixed where it brokeWhich is usually the part nobody plans for, and the part that never shows up in a mockup.
  4. Then it gets a listingA cover, a preview of every page, and a description of what actually prints.

A resource that has not survived a class period is not ready to sell to somebody else's class period.

What you can expect

Four things that are true of every file, or it does not get listed.

  • Real page previews. Every listing shows what actually prints.
  • Editable text, every time, so the names and the wording end up being yours.
  • Color and an ink saving black and white version in the same file.
  • A straight answer if you write to us about a file. A person reads it.
